# UI/UX Audit — Landing Page
_Visual audit conducted 2026-03-22 via live browser review (localhost:5173)_

---

## HIGH IMPACT

### 1. Hero background is visually noisy
The fire/smoke texture with floating icons (house, compass) competes with the headline. The icons are barely visible and add no meaning. The hero is the highest-stakes section — it deserves a cleaner, more intentional treatment. Consider a simpler dark background with a subtle texture or a single strong visual element rather than ambient decorative noise.

### 2. Section color transitions are jarring
The warm red hero → different-dark-red beer section → near-black navy for charts feels like three different sites. There's no visual continuity. A gradual color progression down the page (or intentional dividers between major acts) would make the narrative feel more cohesive and intentional.

### 3. Permanent Collection guide cards are understyled
These are the site's main action — yet they're the least visually compelling section on the page. Plain dark cards with small emoji icons. Given the museum framing, these deserve more visual weight: a framed/exhibit treatment, subtle glow on hover, or a richer card design that matches the quality of the content above them.

---

## MEDIUM IMPACT

### 4. Dollar Calculator — confusing right-column percentages
The right column shows numbers like `9.3%`, `0.8%`, `649x` next to each item with no clear label explaining what the percentage means. These need a column header or inline explanation.

### 5. Great Divergence chart thumbnails feel cramped
The four small chart preview cards sitting above the main carousel are visually busy. The section jumps straight into information before the visitor is oriented. A cleaner entry — headline, one sentence, then the carousel — would breathe better.

### 6. Side navigation labels are awkward
The floating section labels on the left edge (e.g., "Monetary Crisis", "The Evidence", "Dollar Calculator") are useful conceptually but visually intrusive — they pop out at odd widths and feel tacked on. Dots-only with tooltips on hover would be cleaner.

---

## LOWER IMPACT

### 7. "What Makes Bitcoin Special?" card colors feel off-palette
The 4 feature cards use brown, dark navy, blue, and purple — colorful but disconnected from the site's orange/amber/dark-navy palette. Bringing these into the palette would unify the visual language.

### 8. Timeline section is very long with no progress signal
The Parallel Genesis timeline is dense. Users have no sense of how much is left. A subtle "scroll to end" indicator or a compact timeline overview at the top would help.
